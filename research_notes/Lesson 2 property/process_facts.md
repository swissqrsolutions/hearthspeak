# Bengaluru: Save → Buy Plot → Build G+2 → Rent Out — Process Facts (researched 2026-11-21)

Purpose: factual backbone for a ~90-min Kannada story course. Each item has a date and a confidence flag.
- **[C]** = confirmed by several news sources
- **[S]** = only one or two secondary sources (blogs, vendors)
- **[K]** = general domain knowledge, not re-verified in this session. Treat it as plausible colour for the story, not as hard fact.

Tool limits: Deccan Herald pages returned 403 to WebFetch, so DH facts come from search snippets. Most numbers come from news and vendor blogs, not from gazette notifications. Before presenting anything as "the law", check it on the official portals: Kaveri 2.0, e-Aasthi, BESCOM, BWSSB, GBA.

## 0. Institutional landscape (2025–26)

- **GBA replaces BBMP [C]:** BBMP was dissolved in early September 2025. The Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) was set up under the Greater Bengaluru Governance Act, 2024, with **5 city corporations: Bengaluru North, South, East, West and Central**. GBA handles big projects, and the corporations handle civic services, khata, tax and plan approvals at their level. Residents were confused for months about which zone or office now serves them, which works well as story material. (DH "Goodbye BBMP, hello GBA: 5 corporations from today", Sept 2025; Brigade blog.)
- **e-Khata (e-Aasthi portal) [C]:** e-Khata has been mandatory for property registration in BBMP/GBA areas since about **Sept–Oct 2024** (sources give 13 Sept or 30 Sept 2024; BDA areas from 13 Sept 2024). Kaveri 2.0, the registration software, is integrated with e-Aasthi. In practice, the **Sub-Registrar will not register a sale deed without a verified e-Khata**. Since **1 July 2025**, e-Khata has also been mandatory for **building plan approvals**. **e-Aasthi 2.0** launched on 21 Sept (probably 2025). All 5 corporations now let you download an e-Khata using the SAS property-tax ID (DH, 2026).
- **Outside GBA (gram panchayat areas such as parts of Hoskote, Anekal, Sarjapur, outer Kengeri):** the equivalent is **e-Swathu** (Form 9 / Form 11A/11B), issued by the gram panchayat. Reports cite "6.5 lakh unauthorised properties around Bengaluru to get e-khata" via e-Swathu, and Minister Khandre's target of "e-Swathu for 8 lakh rural properties by Dec 31". [S]
- **BDA** (Bangalore Development Authority) develops and sells its own layouts. **BMRDA** and **BIAAPA** (Bangalore International Airport Area Planning Authority) approve private layouts in the outer region.

- In listings, **premiums** for east-facing, corner or park-facing plots typically add ₹200–300/sq ft. One Hoskote listing said "3,300 to 3,600/sq ft based on premium charges like East facing / corner plot / Park view". [S] BDA auctions corner sites separately because they fetch more (DH, 2025: "133 corner sites up for e-auction"; Aug 2025: 122 corner/intermediate sites e-auctioned from 11 Aug).
- **Story-realistic budget:** a 30×40 plot in an approved layout on the outskirts costs **₹35–55 lakh**. Add about 7.6% for stamp duty and registration, ~1% broker fee, and legal verification (₹10–25k [K]).

### Loans [S]
- Banks fund only plots in layouts approved by **BDA/BMRDA/BIAAPA** etc. A plot on revenue land or in an unapproved layout is usually rejected outright. The bank checks the plot number against the approved layout plan.
- **Plot-loan LTV is roughly 65–75%** (some lenders say 75–90%). Home or construction loans run up to 80–90% for an A-khata property. The buyer must pay **25–35% of the plot cost as down payment**, plus all stamp duty (banks do not finance stamp duty [K]).
- **B-khata:** most nationalised banks decline it. Some NBFCs lend up to about 55% of value, at higher interest.
- **Composite loan (plot + construction):** the bank pays the plot money first, then releases construction money in stages (e.g. plinth, slabs, finishing). **Construction must usually start within about 3 years.**
- Lenders typically want a **CIBIL score around 750+** [K]. Tax benefit: interest on a plot-only loan earns no deduction until a house is built [K].
- Home-loan rates in 2026 are about 7.25–8.5% (SBI RLLR around 8.15% per a site banner, 2026). [S]

## 2. Plot types & due diligence

### Types (most to least safe) [K + C]
1. **BDA site:** allotted or auctioned by BDA. You get an allotment letter, a lease-cum-sale deed, then an absolute sale deed. Title is clear. Problem: **NPKL (Nadaprabhu Kempegowda Layout)** suffered years of litigation over land acquisition and slow infrastructure (DH coverage).
2. **BMRDA/BIAAPA/Local Planning Authority-approved private layout:** the land has DC conversion, the layout plan is approved, and civic-amenity (CA) sites and parks are relinquished to the authority. Plots are released in stages (e.g. 40% / 60% release order).
3. **Revenue site / B-khata:** agricultural land subdivided without conversion or layout approval. It is cheap, banks will not lend on it, and since April 2025 you have struggled to get power or water connections.

### A-khata vs B-khata [C]
- **A-khata:** the property is fully compliant (approved layout, converted land, betterment fees paid). Banks lend on it and building plans can be sanctioned.
- **B-khata:** a register of irregular properties. Tax is collected, but the property is not regularised.
- **2025 conversion scheme:** GBA launched a one-time B-to-A khata conversion around Oct 2025, with a 100-day drive from **1 Nov 2025**. The fee is **5% of guidance value**, after a site inspection, documents and CAD drawings. Example: a 30×40 site at a guidance value of ₹7,000/sq ft pays **₹4.2 lakh**. Response was lukewarm, with only 1,169 applications in the first week. About 7.5 lakh B-khata properties exist.
- **"Bhoo Guarantee" (announced 13 May 2026, the state's "6th guarantee"):** the fee was cut to **2% of guidance value** for a 100-day window from **16 May to about 23/24 Aug 2026**. It covered about 23 lakh properties (16 lakh A-khata and 7 lakh B-khata). Saturday e-khata camps ran under the slogan **"ನನ್ನ ಖಾತೆ, ನನ್ನ ಹಕ್ಕು" (Nanna Khata, Nanna Hakku)**. After 24 Aug 2026 the fee reportedly went back to 5% (NoBroker only, so [S]).

### Documents to check [K, standard practice]
- **Mother deed / title chain for 30 years** (some lawyers ask for 40). Every sale deed in the chain should be checked.
- **EC (Encumbrance Certificate, ಋಣಭಾರ ಪ್ರಮಾಣಪತ್ರ)** for 13–30 years. **Form 15** means transactions are recorded. **Form 16** means a "nil" encumbrance. You can now get it online via Kaveri 2.0.
- **RTC / Pahani (ಪಹಣಿ)** for the original agricultural land, plus mutation records (MR) to trace how the land changed hands.
- **DC conversion order** (land converted from agricultural to non-agricultural use).
- **Layout approval plan + release order** from BMRDA, BIAAPA or BDA.
- **Survey sketch**, plus **Form 11E / Podi** (sub-division) for the survey number.
- **Khata certificate + khata extract (e-Khata)**, property-tax paid receipts, and the **possession certificate** (for BDA or society sites).
- For a BDA site: allotment letter, possession certificate and **absolute sale deed**.
- **Legal opinion from an advocate** (₹5k–25k [K]). A bank's empanelled lawyer also does title verification if you take a loan.

### Common frauds / broker tricks [K + C]
- A **B-khata or revenue site sold as A-khata**, or a "BMRDA approval applied" layout sold as approved.
- **Double registration:** the same site sold to two buyers. This is less common now with e-Khata, which gives each property a unique ID, but it still happens.
- **GPA (General Power of Attorney) sales:** you buy from a "power holder", not the owner. The Supreme Court's 2011 Suraj Lamp ruling says a GPA sale does not transfer title.
- **Missing DC conversion**, or a site sitting in a **lake buffer zone, raja kaluve (storm-water drain) buffer** or under a high-tension line. Such buildings risk demolition, as in the 2024 BBMP drives against encroachments.
- **Civic-amenity sites or parks sold as residential plots.**
- **Fake ECs or e-Khatas.** Check them on the official Kaveri and e-Aasthi portals yourself.
- Payment trick: "Pay the full amount in cash and register the deed at guidance value." The cash part is black money and leaves you with no legal proof of payment.

## 3. Broker & site visit [K, mostly unsourced]
- **Commission:** usually **1–2% of the plot price**, often paid by both buyer and seller. Rental brokers commonly charge **one month's rent** (or 15 days). I found no reliable source for these figures; they are typical market practice.
- **Questions to ask on a site visit (useful dialogue):**
  - "How wide is the road: 30 ft or 40 ft?" (Road width limits how many floors you can build.)
  - "Is there UGD (underground drainage) and a Cauvery water line?"
  - "Does it flood in the monsoon?"
  - "How far is the lake? Is it in the buffer zone?"
  - "Is there an HT line overhead?"
  - "Which way does it face? East?" (Vastu: east- and north-facing plots fetch a premium; south-facing plots sell cheaper.)
  - "Corner site?" (premium ₹200–500/sq ft)
  - "Is it A-khata or B-khata? Show me the e-Khata."
  - "Is the layout BMRDA-approved? Show me the release order."
  - "How many plots in the layout are already built on?"

## 4. Purchase

### Costs (Karnataka) [C]
- **Stamp duty: 5%** for property worth over ₹45 lakh. It is 2% up to ₹20 lakh and 3% between ₹20 and 45 lakh. Some sources disagree on whether these lower slabs apply to plots.
- **Registration fee: doubled from 1% to 2%, effective 31 Aug 2025**, the first revision since 2003.
- **Cess + surcharge: ~0.5–0.6%.** The total is about **7.5–7.6%** of the **higher of guidance value and sale price**.
- **Guidance value (ಮಾರ್ಗಸೂಚಿ ದರ)** is the government's minimum rate. A revision was mooted for April 2026; I could not confirm whether it was notified.
- **Worked example:** a ₹40 lakh plot pays 3% stamp duty (₹1.2 lakh), 2% registration (₹80k) and about 0.5% cess (₹20k), roughly ₹2.2 lakh in total. At ₹50 lakh the total is about ₹3.8 lakh.

### Steps [K + C]
1. Negotiate the price, then sign a **sale agreement (ಕ್ರಯ ಕರಾರು / sale agreement)** and pay an **advance of 10–20%**. The agreement usually gives 60–90 days to complete the sale. Lawyers advise putting a refund clause in it.
2. Get a lawyer's title verification. The bank does its own legal and technical verification if you take a loan.
3. Book a slot on **Kaveri 2.0 (kaveri.karnataka.gov.in)**, upload the draft deed and pay stamp duty and fees online.
4. Go to the **Sub-Registrar Office (ಉಪನೋಂದಣಾಧಿಕಾರಿ ಕಚೇರಿ)**. You need **buyer, seller and 2 witnesses**, plus Aadhaar e-KYC, photos and biometrics. Pay the balance by DD or RTGS. The **sale deed (ಕ್ರಯಪತ್ರ)** is registered.
5. **Khata transfer / mutation** to your name on e-Aasthi (GBA areas) or e-Swathu (panchayat areas). Kaveri 2.0 and e-Aasthi integration auto-triggers part of this. Then pay property tax in your own name.
- **TDS:** if the plot costs ₹50 lakh or more, the buyer deducts **1% TDS** (Section 194-IA). [K]

## 5. Building approvals

- **Building plan sanction (ನಕ್ಷೆ ಮಂಜೂರಾತಿ):** applied for through the GBA/corporation online system by a **Council of Architecture-registered architect**. e-Khata has been mandatory since 1 July 2025. Under the earlier BBMP rule, a plan with no objection in 30 working days counts as "deemed approved". [S]
- **"Nambike Nakshe" (ನಂಬಿಕೆ ನಕ್ಷೆ, "trust map")** was launched around Sept 2024 for **sites up to 4,000 sq ft (e.g. 50×80)** in BBMP limits. A licensed architect or engineer certifies the plan, the owner uploads it, and an instant provisional approval follows. It allows houses of up to 4 floors. A related scheme, **"Suvarna Paravanige"**, covers residential plans up to 4,000 sq ft (single basement + ground + up to 3 upper floors, up to 4 dwelling units, height up to 15 m) via the zonal ADTP. I could not confirm whether Nambike Nakshe continues under GBA in 2026. [S]
- **FAR (Floor Area Ratio):** **1.75** for small plots on roads up to about 12 m (40 ft), under RMP-2015 zoning regulations. For a 1,200 sq ft plot this allows about **2,100 sq ft total built-up area** (unofficial summary). [S]
- **Setbacks:** the old rule was percentages of plot dimensions (about 12% front, 8% rear, 8% each side). In 2025 Karnataka proposed uniform setback rules for small plots and an RMP amendment allowing **stilt + 3 floors (4 floors including stilt) on 30×40 plots, with at most 4 kitchens**. An expert noted that the unchanged FAR of 1.75 keeps this "on paper". A later item says "up to 6 flats allowed on 60×40 sites". [S]
- **Real-world practice:** many people build G+2 or G+3 on 30×40 plots, exceeding sanctioned FAR. These deviations are exactly what caused the 2025 OC and power-connection trouble.
- **Commencement certificate:** required before work starts. You must notify the authority at the plinth stage. [K]

### Occupancy Certificate (OC) saga — key story conflict [C]
- **17 Dec 2024:** the **Supreme Court** ruled that utilities should give water, power and sewer connections only after a completion or occupancy certificate. It told states to stop connections to buildings built without approved plans.
- **13 Mar 2025:** a **KERC circular** told BESCOM and other ESCOMs to comply. BESCOM **stopped permanent connections without OC/CC from about 4 Apr 2025**. About **50,000 connections stalled in the city, over 1 lakh across BESCOM's area**. MSMEs were also hit. Reports followed of **power theft rising** and families living for months on temporary construction connections.
- **9 Sept 2025 government order:** **no OC needed for residential buildings on plots up to 1,200 sq ft with an approved plan, up to G+2 or stilt+3**, under Section 241(7) of the GBA Act. This was expected to free about **33,000** families' connections, but about 60,000 BESCOM and 14,000 BWSSB applications stayed stuck.
- **2025:** reports of a new state law allowing power and sanitary connections to buildings without plan sanction (DH 3743476; details unclear).
- **June 2026:** the exemption was widened to **residential plots up to 2,400 sq ft (+20% deviation), up to G+3 / S+4**. A **22 June 2026 order** opened a **one-time 15-day window**: buildings completed, or with a temporary connection, by **31 May 2026** could apply at the BESCOM sub-division office. Officials geo-tag photos in the owner's presence. About **3 lakh** applications were pending statewide, about **1–1.5 lakh** of them with BESCOM. Commercial buildings and PGs are not covered. [S/C]
- **Story takeaway:** in 2025 a hero with a 30×40 G+2 house and an approved plan would have been stuck from April to September 2025, then relieved by the 1,200 sq ft exemption. If the hero built beyond the plan (say G+3), they would have waited until the June 2026 window.

## 6. BESCOM (electricity)
- **Apply** online via the BESCOM portal or the **BESCOM Mithra app**, or at the **sub-division office (AEE/AE)**.
- **Documents:** application form, ownership proof (sale deed / e-Khata), ID (Aadhaar / voter ID / PAN), sanctioned plan, and later the OC or exemption. A **licensed electrical contractor** must submit the wiring test report. [S + K]
- **Temporary connection for construction:** usually 3-phase, around 5–10 kW. Since **Feb 2025** (urban) and **1 July 2025** (rural), **smart meters have been mandatory for new and temporary connections**, and **prepaid** meters for temporary ones (minimum ₹100 recharge, power cuts off automatically at zero balance). You buy the meter from a BESCOM-approved vendor: about **₹4,998 single-phase, about ₹8,880 three-phase**. A **High Court interim order on 25 Apr 2025** restrained mandating smart meters; BESCOM then told the court smart meters apply only to new houses. The status is murky; verify before writing.
- **Permanent connection:** one meter per rental unit, so a G+2 with 3–6 units needs 3–6 meters. A typical load is **3–5 kW per 2BHK**. Fixed charge is about **₹120/kW** (residential, tariff-dependent). You pay service-line charges, an initial security deposit and a meter security deposit after sanction.
- **Meter board:** grouped meters on a common wall or in a meter room near the gate, so BESCOM staff can read them without entering. Larger loads may need a transformer. [K]
- **Timeline (Citizen Charter):** feasibility and sanction letter within 7 days, connection within 30 days if papers are in order. In practice, the 2025 OC freeze stretched this to months.
- **Typical problems:** lineman or AE asking for "speed money", pole shifting, transformer capacity in new layouts, OC demands, and smart-meter cost disputes. [K]

## 7. BWSSB (water + sewerage)
- **Apply** online (BWSSB portal / Sakala). You need the sanctioned plan, e-Khata, a licensed plumber and proof of rainwater harvesting (RWH). [S]
- **Pro-rata charge:** about **₹400 per sq m of built-up area (residential)** and ₹600 for non-residential. For a G+2 house it may apply only from a certain floor up; sources conflict.
- **Beneficiary Capital Contribution (BCC):** applies in the **110 villages** added to the city in 2007. It ranges from about ₹5,000 (house up to 600 sq ft) to around ₹30,000, and older slabs gave ₹8,000 for a 1,200 sq ft site. **A Karnataka High Court ruling (WP 17661/2025) reportedly held BCC and GBWASP charges illegal but upheld pro-rata.** Status of any appeal is unknown.
- **EMI option:** BWSSB offered a **12-month EMI plan** for new Cauvery connections (Constructionworld). **Cauvery Stage V** (commissioned Oct 2024) brought water to the 110 villages, and BWSSB ran connection drives. [K/S]
- **Rainwater harvesting (ಮಳೆನೀರು ಕೊಯ್ಲು):** mandatory under **Section 72-A of the BWSSB (Amendment) Act, 2009** for sites of **1,200 sq ft and above (30×40)** for new buildings, and for **60×40 and above for old buildings** (2021 amendment). The **penalty is about 50% extra on the water bill** for domestic users for the first 3 months, then 100%. Commercial users pay 100%, then 200%. In 2024 over 39,000 homes were paying this penalty regularly.
- **Borewells:** the **Karnataka Ground Water (Regulation & Control) Act 2011 / Rules 2012** apply. You need **Karnataka Ground Water Authority (KGWA) permission** in notified areas and must use a **KGWA-registered drilling agency**, and register existing borewells. During the **March 2024 water crisis**, permission was made compulsory for new borewells in Bengaluru Urban. Fines range from ₹5,000 up to ₹10,000 and/or imprisonment; sources conflict. Most new houses in the outskirts still depend on a borewell plus tanker water at ₹800–1,500 per 6,000 L load, and tanker prices spiked during the 2024 crisis. [S/K]

## 8. Construction
- **Architect:** about 2–5% of construction cost, or a flat ₹30–80/sq ft for plan and approval drawings. **Structural engineer:** ₹15–30k for a small house. Soil test: ₹10–25k. [K]
- **Turnkey (material + labour) rates in 2025–26** [S]: ₹1,840–2,510/sq ft (Bajaj, 2025); ₹1,995 / 2,145 / 2,495 / 2,750 for standard / classic / premium / royale packages (Brick & Bolt, including GST); ₹2,000–2,800 standard RCC (InfraLens 2026). **Story figure: ₹2,200/sq ft standard.** A G+2 of about 3,000 sq ft costs around **₹66 lakh**; 2,100 sq ft (FAR-compliant) costs around **₹46 lakh**.
- **Labour-only contract:** about ₹450–750/sq ft. One calculator gives ₹725/sq ft for labour; the owner buys all materials. The **mestri (ಮೇಸ್ತ್ರಿ, mason foreman)** brings the gang. Labour often comes from North Karnataka (Raichur, Yadgir) or from Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal. [K]
- **Material prices (2025–26)** [S]:
  - Cement OPC 53 at ₹390–476 per 50 kg bag; PPC at ₹360–433.
  - TMT Fe500D at ₹58–78/kg; reported jump from ₹55–60k to ₹64–73k per tonne.
  - **M-sand** at ₹1,100–1,800 per tonne (plastering sand ₹1,300–1,700). River sand is largely banned or scarce in Karnataka, so M-sand is standard.
  - Wire-cut red bricks at ₹8–11 each; 4" AAC blocks at ₹48–59; 6" or 8" solid concrete blocks are common [K].
- **Sequence and typical 10–14 month timeline** [K]:
  1. Soil test, then **bhoomi puja (ಭೂಮಿ ಪೂಜೆ)** on an auspicious muhurta. The northeast (Ishanya) corner is dug first per Vastu.
  2. Footings and plinth.
  3. Columns and the ground-floor slab. The slab is cured for **14–21 days** with water ponding, and shuttering stays for about 14 days.
  4. First-floor and second-floor slabs, then the headroom and water tank.
  5. Brickwork, plumbing and electrical conduits, plaster, flooring, painting.
  6. **Gruhapravesha (ಗೃಹಪ್ರವೇಶ)**, the housewarming.
- **Payment milestones** (turnkey): booking 5–10%, foundation 15%, each slab 10–15%, brickwork, plaster, flooring and handover in stages. [K]
- **Delays and pitfalls** [K]:
  - **Monsoon (June–Oct)** delays.
  - Labour leaving for festivals (Ugadi, Diwali, Chhath) or for harvest.
  - Material price hikes; cost overruns of 10–25% are common.
  - **Tanker water** for curing.
  - Contractors quoting a low rate and then charging for "extras".
  - Disputes over cement brand, steel thickness and number of bags.
  - Neighbour disputes over setbacks; officials or ward "local leaders" seeking money for not reporting deviations.
- **Quality checks** [K]: cube test for concrete, slab thickness, steel spacing (the engineer inspects before every pour), and plumb and level checks. Waterproof the terrace before the monsoon.

## 9. Renting
- **Deposit norm:** traditionally **6–10 months' rent** in Bengaluru, often 10 for apartments. Viral 2025 posts (e.g. ₹23 lakh deposit on a 4BHK at ₹2.3 lakh/month) made it a running joke. In the outskirts and independent houses, 3–6 months is common now [K]. **Zero-deposit** options are rising, with tenants paying up to 10% more rent.
- **Legal cap? Disputed.** Several blogs claim the **Karnataka Rent (Amendment) Act 2025/2026 caps deposits at 2 months** and requires Kaveri 2.0 registration (₹5,000 penalty). **Khaitan & Co (Apr 2026)** says the amendment (Act 7 of 2026, gazetted 8 Jan 2026) changed only §54 penalties and **did not cap deposits**. Karnataka has **not adopted the Model Tenancy Act**. My conclusion is that there is **no enforced cap**; avoid stating one in the story.
- **Agreement:** an **11-month rental agreement (ಬಾಡಿಗೆ ಕರಾರು)** on **e-stamp paper**, digitally stamped since 1 July 2025. Duty is **₹200** per one guide, or **0.5% of (annual rent + deposit), capped at ₹500** per others; ₹500 is the most cited. Agreements of 11 months or less do not have to be registered. [S]
- **Police tenant verification:** a Bengaluru City Police directive. Forms are on the KSP website or at Bangalore One centres, and the tenant gives Aadhaar and photos. The landlord's liability is "recommended / landlord's duty"; penalties are not clearly enforced. [S]
- **Typical rents in the outskirts (2025)** [S]:
  - 1BHK: **₹5,500–10,000** in Hoskote and Kengeri; ₹8,000–15,000 in Electronic City.
  - 2BHK: **₹15,000–20,000** in Kengeri; ₹20,000–30,000 in Electronic City.
  - For a G+2 on 30×40 with, say, 2×2BHK + 2×1BHK, expect roughly **₹45,000–60,000/month** in total.
- **Tenant preferences and discrimination:** "**Vegetarians only**", "**No bachelors**", "**family only**", and bias by religion, caste or diet (non-veg) are widely reported (The Logical Indian: "Bachelors not allowed"). Landlords also screen out pets and favour people from their own language group. In Bengaluru, a "Kannada-speaking landlord vs north-Indian tenant" language tension is a running theme on social media. [C/K]
- **Maintenance:** painting deductions from the deposit on move-out (usually one month's rent) are a common dispute (Hans India, 2025: tenants frustrated by unexplained deductions). Other recurring jobs: sump and tank cleaning, motor repair, and BESCOM bill splitting with sub-meters.
- **Tax** [K]:
  - Rent is taxed as "Income from house property", with a 30% standard deduction on net annual value and a deduction for home-loan interest (no ₹2 lakh cap for let-out property, but set-off against other income is limited to ₹2 lakh).
  - A tenant paying **over ₹50,000/month** must deduct **TDS at 2%** (Section 194-IB; 5% before 1 Oct 2024). Section numbers may change under the **Income Tax Act 2025, in force from 1 Apr 2026**.
  - **Property tax** goes to GBA. Paying by about April–May earns a 5% rebate (deadline extended to 31 May in 2026).
