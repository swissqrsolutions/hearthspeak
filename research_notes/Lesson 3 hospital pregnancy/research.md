## 0. Story skeleton suggestions (for the lesson writer)

- Family: **Ajji** (ಅಜ್ಜಿ, 72, so eligible for 70+ cover), her son and daughter-in-law, and the grandson's wife **Divya**, who is pregnant. If the baby is born, Ajji becomes **ಮುತ್ತಜ್ಜಿ (muttajji, great-grandmother)**. That gives the story an emotional arc: "I want to see my great-grandchild."
- Arc A (heart): chest pain at night → 108 call → private hospital ER (or Jayadeva) → ECG → cath lab → angiogram shows blockage → angioplasty vs bypass decision → consent → ICU → insurance desk drama → discharge → rehab, diet, walking.
- Arc B (pregnancy): positive test → gynaecologist → NT scan → anomaly scan → glucose test → seemantha in the 7th month → move to tavaru-mane → labour → normal vs C-section → naming on the 11th day → vaccines.
- Natural crossover: Ajji's ICU stay overlaps with Divya's anomaly scan. Ajji insists on attending the seemantha after discharge, and the doctor sets limits.

## 1. Real procedures and numbers (Bengaluru/Karnataka, 2025–26)

### 1A. Emergency and cardiac care

**108 Arogya Kavacha ambulance** [V]
- Free, state-run, dial **108** (112 is the national all-emergency number). Karnataka's fleet is **715 ambulances (about 484 BLS and 231 ALS)**. 262 replacements were flagged off in Nov 2023. A 2025 report found **only about 530 available**, with about 25% under maintenance. The design benchmark is a **20-minute response**. A CAG audit found the 30-minute standard was met in only 72% of cases.
  Sources: Deccan Herald, "Karnataka launches 262 ambulances…" (Nov 2023), https://deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fkarnataka-launches-262-ambulances-under-108-service-fleet-now-715-2791919 ; DH, "25% of Karnataka's 108 ambulances under maintenance" (late 2025), https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/25-of-karnatakas-108-ambulances-under-maintenance-sparking-emergency-response-fears-3845123
- The state launched a **centralised command and control centre** with GPS tracking, computer-aided dispatch and automatic caller location (ThePrint, 2025/26). DH reported the **state was set to take over 108 from the private operator by Feb 2026**, with 175 new ambulances planned [V that it was planned; ? whether completed].
  https://theprint.in/india/karnataka-launches-centralised-command-and-control-centre-for-emergency-ambulance-services/2941051/ ; https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fkarnataka-govt-set-to-take-over-108-emergency-services-by-february-2026-3794018
- Story realism: callers often get a call-back from the EMT, are asked for a landmark ("ಹತ್ರ ಏನಿದೆ? ಲ್ಯಾಂಡ್‌ಮಾರ್ಕ್ ಹೇಳಿ"), and families frequently **take an Ola/Uber/auto or a neighbour's car** instead because of Bengaluru traffic. Both choices are realistic. [K]

**Dr. Puneeth Rajkumar Hrudaya Jyothi Yojane (STEMI hub-and-spoke)** [V]
- Taluk and district hospitals (spokes) do an AI-read ECG (Tricog) within minutes and give **Tenecteplase (clot-dissolving injection) free**. A private dose costs about ₹25–45k. The patient is then shifted to a **Jayadeva hub** (Bengaluru, Mysuru, Kalaburagi). By 2025 there were **86 spoke hospitals**. Mysuru data to 31 May 2025: 1,00,617 ECGs, 644 heart attacks diagnosed.
  https://starofmysore.com/over-1-lakh-needy-people-treated-under-dr-puneeth-rajkumar-hrudaya-jyothi-scheme-in-mysuru/amp ; https://thesouthfirst.com/health/karnataka-steps-up-heart-health-initiatives-public-places-aeds-hub-spoke-model/
- Named after the Kannada film star Puneeth Rajkumar, who died of a cardiac arrest in Oct 2021 at 46. Any Kannadiga learner's interlocutor will know this, which makes it a good cultural note. [K]

Room rent is extra, and investigations add ₹2k–10k. - Medical facts for the doctor's explanation [K]: angiogram = dye test to *see* the blockage; angioplasty = balloon + stent through the wrist (radial) or groin; 1–2 vessels → usually stent; **3-vessel or left-main disease, especially with diabetes → bypass is often advised**. No lifting and no driving for ~1 week after angioplasty and ~6 weeks after bypass. Cardiac rehab is 6–12 weeks of supervised walking.

**Private health insurance: cashless process** 
- **IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance (29 May 2024)**: the insurer must decide a cashless request **within 1 hour**, and **final discharge authorisation within 3 hours**. Any extra hospital charges from a delay beyond 3 hours are borne by the insurer [V for 1 h/3 h; K for the penalty]. Lok Sabha data: 86.88% of pre-auths were done within 1 hour (Aug 2024–May 2025) [V].
  https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/insurance/how-long-does-cashless-health-insurance-claim-authorisation-take-latest-irdai-rules-and-data/article-185553 ; https://www.policybazaar.com/health-insurance/general-info/news/no-more-waiting-irdai-mandates-cashless-claim-within-hour
- Steps (desk reality) [K, consistent with Policybazaar/Moneylife]:
  1. At the TPA/insurance desk: show the health card or e-card, policy copy, Aadhaar and PAN.
  2. The hospital sends the pre-authorisation form with the doctor's notes and estimate to the TPA or insurer.
  3. The insurer gives **initial approval**, often a partial amount, and the hospital may still ask for a **deposit/advance**.
  4. Enhancements are sent if the treatment changes (angiogram → angioplasty → bypass).
  5. At discharge, the final bill goes for final approval and the family pays **non-payables + co-pay + deductions**.
  6. **Approval at admission doesn't guarantee payment.** Claims can still be denied at discharge, for example for non-disclosure of earlier diabetes or BP [V Mint/Moneylife].
- Deduction vocabulary for the story:
  - **Room-rent cap**: typically 1% of sum insured per day for a room and 2% for ICU [K].
  - **Proportionate deduction**: if you take a costlier room, associated charges are cut in the same ratio. Commentary on the 2024 circular says ICU, pharmacy, implants and diagnostics are exempt from this cut [V secondary, conflicting; mark ?].
  - **Co-pay**: senior-citizen policies often carry 10–30% co-pay [K].
  - **Non-payables/consumables**: gloves, PPE, syringes and admin charges, unless the policy has a consumables add-on [K].
  - **Waiting period for pre-existing disease**: now max 3 years under the 2024 rules [K].
  - **Reimbursement route**: pay first, then file the claim with the discharge summary, bills, reports and a cancelled cheque, usually within 15–30 days of discharge [K].
- **ICU visiting rules** [K, typical private-hospital practice, varies]: 1 attender pass; visiting slots such as 11–12 and 5–6 pm; 1–2 people at a time; no children; remove footwear and sanitise; the doctor briefs relatives once a day ("counselling time"). Most hospitals expect **one family member to stay as "attender"** at all times.
- **Consent** [K]:
  - Separate consents are taken for the angiogram, the angioplasty ("we may proceed to angioplasty in the same sitting"), anaesthesia, and blood transfusion. A **"high-risk consent"** is used for elderly or critical patients.
  - The patient signs if conscious; otherwise the next of kin does, with a witness.
  - Forms are usually bilingual (Kannada and English), and families sign in a hurry.
- **DNR / end-of-life** [K]: The Supreme Court's 2018 Common Cause judgment recognised living wills and passive euthanasia, and the procedure was simplified in Jan 2023. Hospitals discuss "DNR"/"DNAR" or "limitation of treatment" with families, and the English term is what's actually used. Keep this offstage or gentle in the lesson; it's realistic for a 70+ patient on ICU admission ("If her heart stops, do you want CPR and ventilator?").
- **Discharge summary** [K]: diagnosis, procedure done (e.g., "PTCA to LAD with 1 DES"), course in hospital, medicines with timings, diet, activity restrictions, warning signs, review date. It's needed for insurance and the next doctor. **Final bill clearance can take 3–6 hours** ("ಡಿಸ್ಚಾರ್ಜ್ ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಅಂದ್ರು, ಸಂಜೆ ಆರಕ್ಕೆ ಆಯ್ತು" is a universal Bengaluru complaint).

### 1B. Pregnancy and newborn

**Antenatal schedule (Government of India STW, ICMR/DHR, Dec 2025)** [V, PDF read]: https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1771568619_ante-natalmanagementofnormalpregnancy-7.pdf
- **Minimum 4 visits** (private gynaecologists do monthly visits to 28 wks, fortnightly to 36, then weekly [K]):
  - **1st visit, first trimester**: LMP → **EDD (due date)**, BP, weight, BMI; Hb, urine, blood group; HIV, VDRL, HBsAg; **DIPSI glucose test (75 g glucose, 2-hour value)**; TSH if high-risk.
    - Optional **aneuploidy screen by USG + double marker**. In private practice this is the **NT scan at 11–14 weeks** [K].
    - Start **folic acid**; **Td (tetanus-diphtheria) dose 1**; fill the **Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card**; register on the RCH portal to get an RCH number.
  - **2nd visit, second trimester**:
    - **Level II (anomaly) USG at 18–20 weeks**.
    - **Repeat DIPSI after 24 weeks** (private: OGTT at 24–28 weeks).
    - **IFA one tablet daily (two if Hb < 11)**.
    - **Calcium 500 mg + vit D twice daily, not taken together with IFA**.
    - **Albendazole 400 mg once** (deworming).
    - **Td dose 2** at least 4 weeks after the first.
    - Birth-preparedness discussion.
  - **3rd visit (28–34 wks) and 4th visit (36–40 wks)**: fundal height, fetal heart, Hb, urine albumin; optional growth scan.
- **Danger signs**: bleeding, leaking fluid, **reduced fetal movement after 28 wks**, severe headache or blurred vision, fever, persistent vomiting, breathlessness.
- **High risk**: age > 35, previous C-section, BP > 140/90, GDM, Hb < 7, twins.
- **PMSMA**: free antenatal check-up by an OBG specialist at government facilities on the **9th of every month** [V]. https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/about-scheme/

**Thayi Card** [V older]: Karnataka's name for the MCP card ("ತಾಯಿ ಕಾರ್ಡ್" = mother card), given at first ANC registration. It records visits and vaccines and is needed for scheme payments. https://www.deccanherald.com/content/471188/madilu-kit-stocks-yet-replenished.html

Not for women in regular government/PSU jobs with paid maternity leave [K]. https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/aug/doc2025825619601.pdf

- **Madilu kit**: Karnataka gives a kit of **about 19 items** to BPL/SC/ST mothers delivering in government hospitals: baby clothes, mosquito net, bedsheet, towel, soap, oil, powder, sanitary pads and so on, worth about ₹1,500. ### 1C. Karnataka customs (pregnancy and birth) [K unless noted; customs vary by community]
- **ಸೀಮಂತ (sīmanta)**, the baby shower, is usually in the **7th month** (sometimes the 5th or 9th; odd months are preferred). Its parts:
  - **ಬಳೆ ಶಾಸ್ತ್ರ** (glass bangles; the jingling is said to soothe the baby);
  - **ಮಡಿಲು ತುಂಬುವುದು** (filling the lap with fruit, coconut, blouse piece, flowers, rice);
  - the woman is dressed in a new saree with jasmine; married women (ಮುತ್ತೈದೆಯರು) give arathi;
  - **ಬಯಕೆ ಊಟ** (a meal of the pregnant woman's cravings; some families hold it as a separate "ಬಯಕೆ ಶಾಸ್ತ್ರ" [?]).
- After seemantha the woman often **goes to her mother's house (ತವರುಮನೆ)** for the first delivery. Her parents traditionally bear delivery costs [K; increasingly negotiated in urban families].
- **ಬಾಣಂತನ (bāṇantana)** is the postpartum confinement: traditionally 40 days to 3 months at the tavaru-mane. Its practices:
  - daily **oil massage and hot-water bath for mother and baby** (often a hired "ಬಾಣಂತಿ ಆರೈಕೆ" lady);
  - **ears and head covered** "so air doesn't enter" (ತಲೆಗೆ ಗಾಳಿ ಹೋಗಬಾರದು);
  - warm, "ಉಷ್ಣ"-balancing foods: dill (ಸಬ್ಬಸಿಗೆ), fenugreek (ಮೆಂತ್ಯ), garlic, ghee, dry ginger, gum/dry-fruit laddu (ಅಂಟಿನ ಉಂಡೆ, esp. North Karnataka), pepper-cumin rasam [V for rasam, a Uttara Kannada poster]. https://wgbis.ces.iisc.ac.in/biodiversity/sahyadri_enews/newsletter/Issue74/posters/Gayatri_Lake%20Poster-2020.pdf
  - **water restriction** for the new mother (a belief doctors actively contest);
  - incense or ajwain smoke (ಹೊಗೆ) for the baby.
- **Pregnancy food beliefs**: **avoid papaya (ಪಪ್ಪಾಯಿ) and pineapple (ಅನಾನಸ್)** because they're "heat" and could cause miscarriage; "eat for two"; "eat ghee so the delivery is smooth"; don't go out during an eclipse (ಗ್ರಹಣ); avoid "ಹಸಿ" (raw) or cold foods; saffron in milk for a fair baby.
  - Doctors' line: ripe papaya in moderation is fine (unripe papaya is the concern); pineapple in normal amounts is fine; eat for one-and-a-bit, not two; control sugar if GDM.
- **ನಾಮಕರಣ (nāmakaraṇa)**:
  - often on the **11th day** (end of birth-impurity, ಸೂತಕ/ಪುರುಡು) or the 21st day; urban families may choose a convenient date;
  - combined with the **ತೊಟ್ಟಿಲು ಶಾಸ್ತ್ರ** (cradle ceremony: baby placed in a decorated cradle, women sing ಜೋಗುಳ/lullabies);
  - father whispers the name into the baby's ear 3 times, and it's written in rice on a plate;
  - name from the nakshatra's starting syllable; the **paternal aunt (ಅತ್ತೆ, father's sister)** often has a role, e.g., naming or cradle duties [?].
- **ದೃಷ್ಟಿ ತೆಗೆಯುವುದು**: removing the evil eye with salt or red chillies circled around the baby; a black dot (ದೃಷ್ಟಿ ಬೊಟ್ಟು) on the cheek.

## 3. How people really talk (model lines, Bengaluru register)

These lines were drafted for this lesson from common Bengaluru hospital and family speech patterns [K]. They aren't quoted from transcripts, so have a native speaker review them before production.

### 3.1 The 108 call
- Caller: "ಹಲೋ, ಆಂಬುಲೆನ್ಸ್ ಬೇಕು, ಬೇಗ! ನಮ್ಮ ಅಜ್ಜಿಗೆ ಎದೆ ನೋವು, ತುಂಬಾ ಬೆವರ್ತಿದ್ದಾರೆ." (I need an ambulance, quick! Grandma has chest pain and is sweating a lot.)
- Operator: "ಗಾಬರಿ ಆಗ್ಬೇಡಿ. ಅಡ್ರೆಸ್ ಹೇಳಿ, ಹತ್ರ ಯಾವುದಾದ್ರೂ ಲ್ಯಾಂಡ್‌ಮಾರ್ಕ್ ಇದ್ಯಾ? ಪೇಶಂಟ್‌ಗೆ ಎಷ್ಟು ವಯಸ್ಸು? ಪ್ರಜ್ಞೆ ಇದ್ಯಾ?" (Don't panic. Tell me the address. Any landmark nearby? Patient's age? Is she conscious?)
- Operator advice: "ಅವರನ್ನ ಕೂರಿಸಿ, ನಡೆಸ್ಬೇಡಿ. ಮನೇಲಿ ಆಸ್ಪಿರಿನ್ ಇದ್ರೆ… ಡಾಕ್ಟ್ರು ಹೇಳಿದ್ರೆ ಮಾತ್ರ ಕೊಡಿ." (Sit her down, don't make her walk. If there's aspirin at home… give it only if the doctor says.) [Aspirin advice varies; keep it conditional.]

### 3.2 ER and doctor's explanation
- Doctor: "ನೀವು ಪೇಶಂಟ್‌ಗೆ ಏನಾಗ್ಬೇಕು?" (How are you related to the patient? This exact formula is universal.)
- "ಇಸಿಜಿ ಮಾಡಿದ್ವಿ. ಹಾರ್ಟ್ ಅಟ್ಯಾಕ್ ಆಗಿದೆ. ತಕ್ಷಣ ಆಂಜಿಯೋಗ್ರಾಮ್ ಮಾಡ್ಬೇಕು." (We did an ECG. It's a heart attack. We need to do an angiogram immediately.)
- Delivering bad news, softly, with a pause before the bad part: "ನೋಡಿ… ನಾನು ನಿಮಗೆ ನಿಜ ಹೇಳ್ಬೇಕು. ಮೂರು ರಕ್ತನಾಳಗಳಲ್ಲೂ ಬ್ಲಾಕ್ ಇದೆ. ಒಂದು ತೊಂಬತ್ತು ಪರ್ಸೆಂಟ್ ಇದೆ." (Look… I have to tell you the truth. All three vessels have blocks. One is 90%.)
- "ಮುಂದಿನ ಇಪ್ಪತ್ನಾಲ್ಕು ಗಂಟೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಮುಖ್ಯ." (The next 24 hours are very important.)
- "ನಾವು ನಮ್ಮ ಕೈಲಾದಷ್ಟು ಪ್ರಯತ್ನ ಮಾಡ್ತೀವಿ." (We'll do everything in our power.)
- "ವಯಸ್ಸಾಗಿದೆ, ಶುಗರ್ ಕೂಡ ಇದೆ, ಅದಕ್ಕೆ ರಿಸ್ಕ್ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ." (She's older and also diabetic, so the risk is a bit higher.)
- Options: "ಎರಡು ಆಪ್ಷನ್ ಇದೆ. ಒಂದು ಸ್ಟೆಂಟ್, ಇನ್ನೊಂದು ಬೈಪಾಸ್. ಅವರ ಕಂಡೀಷನ್ ನೋಡಿದ್ರೆ ಬೈಪಾಸ್ ಒಳ್ಳೇದು ಅಂತ ನನ್ನ ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಯ. ಆದ್ರೆ ನಿರ್ಧಾರ ನಿಮ್ದು." (There are two options: stent or bypass. Given her condition, my opinion is bypass is better. But the decision is yours.)
- Reassuring: "ಭಯ ಪಡೋ ಅಗತ್ಯ ಇಲ್ಲ, ಈ ಪ್ರೊಸೀಜರ್ ನಾವು ದಿನಾ ಮಾಡ್ತೀವಿ." (No need to be scared, we do this procedure every day.) / "ಈಗ ಸ್ಟೇಬಲ್ ಆಗಿದ್ದಾರೆ." (She's stable now.)
- Asking consent: "ಈ ಫಾರ್ಮ್‌ನಲ್ಲಿ ರಿಸ್ಕ್‌ಗಳೆಲ್ಲಾ ಬರೆದಿದೆ. ಓದಿ, ಏನಾದ್ರೂ ಡೌಟ್ ಇದ್ರೆ ಕೇಳಿ, ಆಮೇಲೆ ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಸೈನ್ ಮಾಡಿ." (All the risks are written in this form. Read it, ask if you have doubts, then sign here.)
- "ಅಮ್ಮಾ, ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಚುಚ್ಚುತ್ತೆ ಅಷ್ಟೇ, ನೋವಾಗಲ್ಲ." (Nurse to the elder: Amma, it'll just prick a little, it won't hurt. Staff address elderly women as ಅಮ್ಮ or ಅಜ್ಜಿ.)

### 3.3 Family's worried questions
- "ಡಾಕ್ಟ್ರೇ, ಅಮ್ಮ ಹುಷಾರಾಗ್ತಾರಾ?" (Doctor, will Amma get better?)
- "ಏನೂ ಪ್ರಾಬ್ಲಮ್ ಇಲ್ಲ ತಾನೇ?" (There's no problem, right?)
- "ಎಷ್ಟು ದಿನ ಐಸಿಯುನಲ್ಲಿ ಇರ್ಬೇಕು?" (How many days in the ICU?)
- "ಒಳಗೆ ಹೋಗಿ ನೋಡ್ಬಹುದಾ? ಒಂದ್ ನಿಮಿಷ ಅಷ್ಟೇ." (Can we go in and see her? Just one minute.)
- "ಒಟ್ಟು ಎಷ್ಟಾಗಬಹುದು ಸರ್? ಎಸ್ಟಿಮೇಟ್ ಕೊಡ್ತೀರಾ?" (Roughly how much in total, sir? Will you give an estimate?)
- "ಅಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ ಹೇಳ್ಬೇಡಿ, ಗಾಬರಿ ಆಗ್ತಾರೆ." (Don't tell Amma, she'll panic. This shielding of elders is very common and a good cultural discussion point.)
- On the phone to relatives: "ಈಗ ಪರ್ವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ, ಸ್ಟೆಂಟ್ ಹಾಕಿದ್ರು. ನಾಳೆ ವಾರ್ಡ್‌ಗೆ ಶಿಫ್ಟ್ ಮಾಡ್ತಾರಂತೆ." (She's okay now, they put a stent in. Apparently they'll shift her to the ward tomorrow.)

### 3.4 Insurance / billing desk
- Desk: "ಇನ್ಶೂರೆನ್ಸ್ ಇದ್ಯಾ? ಕಾರ್ಡ್, ಆಧಾರ್, ಪಾಲಿಸಿ ಕಾಪಿ ಕೊಡಿ." (Do you have insurance? Give me the card, Aadhaar and policy copy.)
- "ಪ್ರಿ-ಆಥರೈಸೇಶನ್ ಕಳ್ಸಿದ್ದೀವಿ, ಟಿಪಿಎ ಇಂದ ಅಪ್ರೂವಲ್ ಬರ್ಬೇಕು. ಅಲ್ಲಿವರೆಗೂ ಐವತ್ತು ಸಾವಿರ ಅಡ್ವಾನ್ಸ್ ಕಟ್ಟಿ." (We've sent the pre-authorisation; approval has to come from the TPA. Until then, pay a ₹50,000 advance.)
- "ನಿಮ್ ಪಾಲಿಸಿಲಿ ರೂಮ್ ರೆಂಟ್ ಲಿಮಿಟ್ ದಿನಕ್ಕೆ ಐದು ಸಾವಿರ. ಸಿಂಗಲ್ ರೂಮ್ ತಗೊಂಡ್ರೆ ಡಿಡಕ್ಷನ್ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ." (Your policy's room-rent limit is ₹5,000 a day. If you take a single room there'll be a deduction.)
- "ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತು ಪರ್ಸೆಂಟ್ ಕೋ-ಪೇ ಇದೆ, ಅದು ನೀವೇ ಕಟ್ಬೇಕು." (There's a 20% co-pay; you have to pay that yourself.)
- "ಕನ್ಸ್ಯೂಮೇಬಲ್ಸ್ ಕವರ್ ಆಗಲ್ಲ." (Consumables aren't covered.)
- Family: "ಕ್ಯಾಶ್‌ಲೆಸ್ ಅಂದ್ರಲ್ಲ, ಮತ್ಯಾಕೆ ದುಡ್ಡು ಕಟ್ಬೇಕು?" (You said cashless, so why do we have to pay?)
- Family: "ಅಪ್ರೂವಲ್ ಇನ್ನೂ ಬಂದಿಲ್ವಾ? ಮೂರು ಗಂಟೆ ಆಯ್ತು!" (Has approval still not come? It's been three hours!)

### 3.5 Discharge and home instructions
- "ಈ ಮಾತ್ರೆ ಯಾವ್ದೇ ಕಾರಣಕ್ಕೂ ನಿಲ್ಲಿಸ್ಬಾರ್ದು." (Don't stop this tablet for any reason. This is the blood thinner.)
- "ದಿನಾ ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಹತ್ತು ನಿಮಿಷ ವಾಕಿಂಗ್, ಆಮೇಲೆ ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ ಮಾಡಿ." (Every morning, a 10-minute walk, then slowly increase it.)
- "ಒಂದು ತಿಂಗಳು ಭಾರ ಎತ್ತಬಾರ್ದು." (No lifting heavy things for a month.)
- "ಮತ್ತೆ ಎದೆ ನೋವು ಬಂದ್ರೆ ಕಾಯ್ಬೇಡಿ, ನೇರ ಎಮರ್ಜೆನ್ಸಿಗೆ ಬನ್ನಿ." (If the chest pain comes back, don't wait; come straight to emergency.)
- "ಉಪ್ಪು, ಎಣ್ಣೆ, ಸಿಹಿ ಕಮ್ಮಿ ಮಾಡಿ." (Cut down on salt, oil and sweets.)

### 3.6 Pregnancy: doctor, family, elders
- Gynaecologist: "ಕಂಗ್ರಾಜುಲೇಶನ್ಸ್! ಆರು ವಾರ ಆಗಿದೆ. ಹಾರ್ಟ್‌ಬೀಟ್ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ." (Congratulations! You're six weeks along. The heartbeat is good.)
- "ಹನ್ನೆರಡನೇ ವಾರದಲ್ಲಿ ಎನ್‌ಟಿ ಸ್ಕ್ಯಾನ್ ಮಾಡಿಸ್ಬೇಕು." (Get the NT scan done in the 12th week.)
- "ಐರನ್ ಮಾತ್ರೆ ಮತ್ತು ಕ್ಯಾಲ್ಸಿಯಂ ಮಾತ್ರೆ ಒಟ್ಟಿಗೆ ತಗೋಬೇಡಿ." (Don't take the iron and calcium tablets together. Straight from the government protocol.)
- "ಮಗು ಒದೀತಿದೆಯಾ? ಮೂವ್‌ಮೆಂಟ್ ಕಮ್ಮಿ ಅನ್ನಿಸಿದ್ರೆ ತಕ್ಷಣ ಬನ್ನಿ." (Is the baby kicking? If movement feels less, come immediately.)
- On a C-section discussion: "ಮಗುವಿನ ಹಾರ್ಟ್‌ರೇಟ್ ಕಮ್ಮಿ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ. ಇನ್ನು ಕಾಯೋದು ಸೇಫ್ ಅಲ್ಲ. ಸಿಸೇರಿಯನ್ ಮಾಡೋಣ ಅಂತ ನನ್ನ ಸಲಹೆ." (The baby's heart rate is dropping. Waiting any longer isn't safe. My advice is a C-section.)
- Relatives: "ಏನಾದ್ರೂ ಒಳ್ಳೆ ಸುದ್ದಿ ಇದ್ಯಾ?" (Any good news?); "ಎಷ್ಟನೇ ತಿಂಗಳು?" (Which month?); "ಹುಡುಗ ಆಗ್ಲಿ, ಹುಡುಗಿ ಆಗ್ಲಿ, ಆರೋಗ್ಯವಾಗಿರ್ಲಿ." (Boy or girl, let it be healthy. This is the polite, PCPNDT-era phrase.)
- Elder (Ajji): "ಪಪ್ಪಾಯಿ ಮುಟ್ಟಬೇಡ, ಉಷ್ಣ." (Don't touch papaya, it's heaty.); "ಇಬ್ಬರಿಗೆ ತಿನ್ನಬೇಕು ಕಣೇ." (You have to eat for two, girl.); "ಗ್ರಹಣದ ದಿನ ಹೊರಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ಬೇಡ." (Don't go out on the eclipse day.); "ಬಾಣಂತಿ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ ನೀರು ಕುಡೀಬಾರ್ದು, ಹೊಟ್ಟೆ ಬರುತ್ತೆ." (A new mother shouldn't drink much water, she'll get a belly.)
  - "ಕಣೇ/ಕಣೋ" is an affectionate Bengaluru/Old Mysore tag, used by elders to younger people.
- Respectful pushback (daughter-in-law / doctor):
  - "ಅಜ್ಜಿ, ನೀವು ಹೇಳೋದು ಸರಿ, ಆದ್ರೆ ಡಾಕ್ಟ್ರು ಹಣ್ಣಾದ ಪಪ್ಪಾಯಿ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ತಿಂದ್ರೆ ತೊಂದ್ರೆ ಇಲ್ಲ ಅಂತ ಹೇಳಿದ್ರು." (Ajji, you're right, but the doctor said a little ripe papaya is no problem.)
  - "ನಿಮ್ಮ ಅನುಭವಾನೂ ಮುಖ್ಯ. ಎರಡೂ ಮಾಡೋಣ: ನಿಮ್ ಸಬ್ಬಸಿಗೆ ಸಾರು, ಡಾಕ್ಟ್ರ ಮಾತ್ರೆ." (Your experience matters too. Let's do both: your dill rasam and the doctor's tablets.)
  - Doctor: "ಅಜ್ಜಿ, ಹಾಲು ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿ ಬರ್ಬೇಕು ಅಂದ್ರೆ ಬಾಣಂತಿ ನೀರು ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿ ಕುಡೀಬೇಕು." (Ajji, if the milk is to come well, the new mother must drink plenty of water.)
- Seemantha talk: "ಸೀಮಂತ ಏಳನೇ ತಿಂಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ಇಟ್ಕೊಳ್ಳೋಣ, ಆಮೇಲೆ ತವರಿಗೆ ಕಳಿಸೋಣ." (Let's hold the seemantha in the 7th month, then send her to her mother's home.); "ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಆಸ್ಪತ್ರೇಲಿದ್ರೂ ಸೀಮಂತ ನಿಲ್ಲಿಸೋದು ಬೇಡ ಅಂತ ಅವರೇ ಹೇಳಿದ್ರು." (Ajji herself said not to cancel the seemantha even though she's in hospital.)
- Birth news: "ಹೆಣ್ಣು ಮಗು! ತಾಯಿ-ಮಗು ಇಬ್ರೂ ಆರಾಮಾಗಿದ್ದಾರೆ." (A baby girl! Mother and baby are both fine. ಆರಾಮ = fine, comfortable, a key Bengaluru word.)

## 4. Grammar opportunities (with lesson-ready examples)

1. **Conditional -ಅರೆ / spoken -ಅ್ರೆ** (ಇದ್ದರೆ → ಇದ್ರೆ; ಆದರೆ → ಆದ್ರೆ):
   - "ಬ್ಲಾಕೇಜ್ ಒಂದೇ ಇದ್ರೆ ಸ್ಟೆಂಟ್ ಹಾಕ್ತೀವಿ; ಮೂರೂ ಇದ್ರೆ ಬೈಪಾಸ್ ಬೇಕಾಗಬಹುದು." (If there's only one blockage we'll put in a stent; if all three, bypass may be needed.)
   - Negative conditional **-ದಿದ್ರೆ**: "ಈಗ ಮಾಡದಿದ್ರೆ ಇನ್ನೊಂದು ಅಟ್ಯಾಕ್ ಆಗಬಹುದು." (If we don't do it now, another attack could happen.)
   - Concessive **-ದ್ರೂ** (even if): "ಆಪರೇಷನ್ ಆದ್ರೂ ಮಾತ್ರೆ ಬಿಡ್ಬಾರ್ದು." (Even after the operation, don't stop the tablets.)
   - Counterfactual **-ಇದ್ರೆ … -ತಿತ್ತು**: "ಅರ್ಧ ಗಂಟೆ ಮುಂಚೆ ಬಂದಿದ್ರೆ ಇನ್ನೂ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿರ್ತಿತ್ತು." (If you'd come half an hour earlier, it would have been even better.) This is the doctor's gentle regret line.
2. **Possibility and probability -ಬಹುದು** (may/might), plus **-ಬಹುದಾ?** for permission:
   - "ರಿಸ್ಕ್ ಇರಬಹುದು", "ಎರಡು-ಮೂರು ದಿನ ಆಗಬಹುದು", "ಒಳಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ಬಹುದಾ?"
   - Teach "ಆಗಬಹುದು" vs "ಆಗುತ್ತೆ" (may vs will) as doctor's hedging.
3. **Instructions, obligation and prohibition**:
   - Polite imperative **-ಇ**: ತಗೊಳ್ಳಿ, ಕೂತ್ಕೊಳ್ಳಿ.
   - Prohibitive **-ಬೇಡಿ / -ಬೇಡ** (informal): ಗಾಬರಿ ಆಗ್ಬೇಡಿ.
   - Obligation **-ಬೇಕು**: ನೀರು ಕುಡೀಬೇಕು.
   - Prohibition-as-rule **-ಬಾರದು / -ಬಾರ್ದು**: ಭಾರ ಎತ್ತಬಾರ್ದು.
   - Sequencers: ಊಟಕ್ಕೆ ಮುಂಚೆ / ಊಟ ಆದ್ಮೇಲೆ (before/after food), ದಿನಕ್ಕೆ ಎರಡು ಸಲ (twice a day). Prescriptions say "1-0-1" and people read it aloud as "ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಒಂದು, ರಾತ್ರಿ ಒಂದು" (one in the morning, one at night).
   - Contrast the formal register: ಮಾಡಿರಿ, ಮಾಡಬಾರದು.
4. **Passive-like forms**:
   - Spoken Kannada avoids the written passive (**-ಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ**: "ಸ್ಟೆಂಟ್ ಅನ್ನು ಇರಿಸಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ", seen on hospital websites and in consent forms).
   - It uses (a) **N + ಆಗು** (intransitive "happen"): "ಆಪರೇಷನ್ ಆಯ್ತು", "ಡಿಸ್ಚಾರ್ಜ್ ಆಗ್ತಾರೆ", "ಹೆರಿಗೆ ಆಯ್ತು"; vs **N + ಮಾಡು** (transitive): "ಡಾಕ್ಟ್ರು ಆಪರೇಷನ್ ಮಾಡಿದ್ರು".
   - (b) The impersonal 3rd plural: "ಸ್ಟೆಂಟ್ ಹಾಕ್ತಾರೆ" (they put a stent).
   - (c) The **causative -ಇಸು** "get something done": ಸ್ಕ್ಯಾನ್ ಮಾಡಿಸಿ, ಟೆಸ್ಟ್ ಮಾಡಿಸಿದ್ವಿ, ಆಸ್ಪತ್ರೆಗೆ ಸೇರಿಸಿದ್ವಿ.
   - This form-vs-speech contrast makes a great register lesson: read a line of the consent form, then say it the spoken way.
5. **Dative experiencer**:
   - "ಅಜ್ಜಿಗೆ ಹಾರ್ಟ್ ಅಟ್ಯಾಕ್ ಆಯ್ತು", "ನನಗೆ ವಾಕರಿಕೆ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ", "ಅವಳಿಗೆ ಏಳು ತಿಂಗಳು" (she's 7 months along), "ಮಗುವಿಗೆ ಕಾಮಾಲೆ".
   - All illness and pregnancy states use the dative. This is central to the lesson.
6. **Reported speech with ಅಂತ** (formal ಎಂದು):
   - "ಡಾಕ್ಟ್ರು ನಾಳೆ ಡಿಸ್ಚಾರ್ಜ್ ಅಂತ ಹೇಳಿದ್ರು." (The doctor said discharge is tomorrow.)
   - "ಭಯ ಪಡ್ಬೇಡಿ ಅಂತ ಹೇಳಿದ್ರು." (He told us not to be afraid.)
   - Embedded question: "ಯಾವಾಗ ಸ್ಕ್ಯಾನ್ ಅಂತ ಕೇಳು." (Ask when the scan is.)
   - Hearsay **-ಅಂತೆ**: "ನಾಳೆ ವಾರ್ಡ್‌ಗೆ ಶಿಫ್ಟ್ ಅಂತೆ." (Apparently she's shifting to the ward tomorrow.) This is perfect for phone calls relaying news to relatives (a game of telephone across the family).
   - Also **ಅಂದ್ರೆ** (that is / if one says): "ಕ್ಯಾಶ್‌ಲೆಸ್ ಅಂದ್ರೆ ದುಡ್ಡು ಕಟ್ಟೋದೇ ಬೇಡ ಅಂತ ಅಲ್ಲ." (Cashless doesn't mean you pay nothing.)
7. **Future and planning**:
   - Non-past (ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ / ಹೋಗ್ತೀವಿ).
   - Hortative **-ಓಣ**: "ತವರಿಗೆ ಏಳನೇ ತಿಂಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ಹೋಗೋಣ" (Let's go to her mother's home in the 7th month.)
   - Intention **-ಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ**: "ಆರು ತಿಂಗಳು ಲೀವ್ ತಗೋಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ." (I'm planning to take six months' leave.)
   - "-ಆದ್ಮೇಲೆ" (after): "ಹೆರಿಗೆ ಆದ್ಮೇಲೆ ಮೂರು ತಿಂಗಳು ಅಲ್ಲೇ ಇರ್ತೀನಿ." (After the delivery I'll stay there three months.)
   - Ordinal **-ನೇ**: ಏಳನೇ ತಿಂಗಳು, ಹನ್ನೊಂದನೇ ದಿನ.
8. **Optative/blessing -ಅಲಿ**: "ಬೇಗ ಹುಷಾರಾಗ್ಲಿ" (may she get well soon), "ಆರೋಗ್ಯವಾಗಿರ್ಲಿ" (may it be healthy), "ಒಳ್ಳೇದಾಗ್ಲಿ" (may good things happen). These are essential social formulas for both arcs.
9. **Tag questions and softeners**: ತಾನೇ? (right?), ಅಲ್ವಾ?, ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ (a bit, as a softener), ಅಷ್ಟೇ (that's all), as in doctors' reassurance: "ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಚುಚ್ಚುತ್ತೆ ಅಷ್ಟೇ" (just a little prick, that's all).
