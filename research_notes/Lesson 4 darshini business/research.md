## 1. Real process and numbers

### 1.1 What a darshini is
- Self-service, vegetarian, **"pay first, eat later"**: pay at the cash counter (ಗಲ್ಲಾ), get a **token**, hand it in at the serving counter, eat **standing** at steel ledges. There is little or no seating and no waiters. [S] Wikipedia
- **Cafe Darshini, Jayanagar, 1983** (R. Prabhakar), inspired by McDonald's/KFC, is generally credited as the first. Several thousand outlets by the 2010s. [S] Wikipedia
- Ownership has historically been dominated by **Udupi–Mangalore coastal families** (Shetty, Kamath, Nayak). [S]
- The economics rest on **high footfall, low ticket size, limited menu, fast turnover (high asset turns)**, and low rent per customer because there is no seating. Darshinis fail less often than restaurants in general. [S] Marcellus/Mint, Jan 2024
- "Darshini" comes from Sanskrit *darśana*, "sight". It is popularly linked to the open kitchen where you watch your food being made. [S]

### 1.3 Funding
- **Savings + family** (often parents' FD, gold loan ಚಿನ್ನ ಅಡವಿಟ್ಟು, or a house mortgage, ಮನೆ ಅಡವಿಟ್ಟು). These are the realistic main sources. [E]
- **PM MUDRA Yojana** (collateral-free, through banks): **Shishu** ≤₹50,000; **Kishore** ₹50k–5 lakh; **Tarun** ₹5–10 lakh; **Tarun Plus** ₹10–20 lakh, only for those who have repaid an earlier Tarun loan (from Oct 2024). A first-timer therefore tops out at **₹10 lakh**. [S] PIB
- **Bank term loan + CGTMSE guarantee**: covers collateral-free MSE loans. The ceiling was raised to ₹10 crore in Budget 2025 (from ₹5 cr) [? not verified this session]. The guarantee fee is paid by the borrower.
- **Udyam registration** (free, online, Aadhaar-based) is needed for MUDRA/MSME benefits. [E/general knowledge]
- Banks want a **project report** (ಯೋಜನಾ ವರದಿ / "project report"), quotations for equipment, a rent agreement and a trade licence. In practice a local CA prepares it. [E]

### 1.4 Legal structure [E, general Indian law]
- **Sole proprietorship**: the default for a single owner. Cheapest, with no separate registration (Udyam + GST + trade licence in the owner's name). Liability is unlimited.
- **Partnership firm**: common among relatives or friends (e.g. owner + a coastal cook-partner). Needs a partnership deed on stamp paper, optionally registered with the Registrar of Firms.
- **LLP**: limited liability, more compliance (MCA filings). Uncommon for a single darshini, more common for chains.
- Rameshwaram Cafe–type chains are private limited companies.

### 1.6 Corruption reality (for the story, realistic but non-instructional)
- Real Lokayukta trap cases involve Bengaluru health and food inspectors. A KG Road circle food inspector demanded **₹1 lakh for a trade licence** and took ₹43,000 in advance, then was caught after a 15 km car chase (DH, year unclear). A BBMP South Zone food safety officer got **3 years' jail for a ₹5,000 bribe** for a licence (TNIE, Dec 2024). A BBMP medical officer was caught taking ₹30,000. GBA officials were caught taking ₹3 lakh for khata work. [S]
- The common pattern [E]: the application is "stuck" or "query raised", an agent (ಏಜೆಂಟ್/ಮಧ್ಯವರ್ತಿ) offers to "get it done", and an inspector hints at "adjusting". Owners refer to this euphemistically as "ಮಾಮೂಲಿ" (the usual) or "ಖರ್ಚು-ವೆಚ್ಚ". The honest route is the online portal, insisting on a Sakala acknowledgement number, and Lokayukta complaints.

### 2.2 Suppliers [E unless marked]
- **Vegetables**: **KR Market (ಕೆ.ಆರ್. ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್)** wholesale before dawn (3–5 a.m.), or a supplier who delivers. Many Jayanagar outlets buy from nearby wholesale shops or Jayanagar complex vendors. Onion/potato by the **sack (ಮೂಟೆ)**. Coconuts by the hundred from a coconut mandi/vendor. Tomato/onion price spikes hit margins.
- **Milk**: **Nandini/KMF**, delivered daily in crates by a KMF dealer/agent. Blue toned ₹54/l from 1 Nov 2026 [S]. Curd from Nandini or homemade.
- **Rice, urad dal (ಉದ್ದಿನ ಬೇಳೆ), rava, sugar, oil**: wholesale traders in **Chickpet / Mamulpet / APMC Yeshwanthpur**, or distributors. Bags of 25/50 kg, usually on a weekly or fortnightly credit cycle (ಉದ್ರಿ / ಕ್ರೆಡಿಟ್).
- **Coffee powder**: a local coffee works (Jayanagar/Basavanagudi coffee roasters), often a 70:30 or 80:20 coffee–chicory blend.
- **LPG**: IOC/HP/BPCL commercial distributor. In March 2026 they were rationing, and black-market cylinders were rumoured [S partially: "distributors warn of hoarding"].
- **Firewood**: fallback during the shortage, ~₹5,000/tonne reported (Mar 2026) [S].

### 2.3 Staff
- A typical small darshini has **8–15 staff** [E]: 1–2 head cooks (ಭಟ್ರು / ಹೆಡ್ ಕುಕ್), dosa master (ದೋಸೆ ಮಾಸ್ಟರ್), helpers (grinding, chopping), coffee/tea man, cashier (often the owner or a family member), counter servers, cleaners/dishwashers.
- **Origins**: traditionally coastal Karnataka/Udupi Brahmin cooks. Increasingly workers come from **North Karnataka**, and many **migrant workers from Assam, Odisha, Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, the Northeast** (85% of Karnataka's migrants come from six states). Owners and staff then speak Hindi + Kannada mix. [S] DH migrant data; [E] darshini-specific mix
- **Wages (legal minimum from 22 May 2026, Greater Bengaluru zone)**: unskilled ₹23,376/month, highly skilled (head chef) ₹31,114. Owners also provide **food + room (ಊಟ-ರೂಮ್)**. Before the hike, actual pay for helpers was often ₹12–18k + food + shared room, and ₹25–40k for a good dosa master/head cook [E]. Hotels say they can't sustain this and some will cut staff [S].
- **Attrition**: staff go home for festivals/harvest and may not return. Poaching by chains ("Rameshwaram is paying more") is common. Salary advances are common, as is unpaid-wage abuse by some employers (NE workers' complaints) [S/E].

### 2.4 Daily schedule [E]
- **Night before**: soak rice and urad dal (ನೆನೆಸು), grind batter (ರುಬ್ಬು) in the evening, leave to ferment overnight.
- **4:30–5:00 a.m.**: cooks arrive, boil milk, make decoction, sambar and chutney, heat oil for vada.
- **6:00/6:30 a.m.**: shutter up. Walkers from Jayanagar parks are the first coffee customers.
- **7:30–10:30**: breakfast rush (idli-vada, dose, bath items).
- **11:30–3:00**: lunch (rice bath items, mini meals).
- **3:30–6:30**: evening snacks (bajji, dose, coffee).
- **Close ~9–10 p.m.**, clean up, grind for tomorrow. Staff often sleep in a room above or behind the hotel.

### 2.5 Payments, delivery, competition
- **UPI dominates** (PhonePe/GPay QR + speaker "soundbox"), and cash is still used. Token printed at the POS. [S] (Rameshwaram uses kiosks + UPI + tokens)
- **Swiggy/Zomato**: commissions **8–28% + ads + payment fees**, and owners say **25–30%** effectively, plus "hidden discounts". One darshini chain owner says he lost ₹50,000 in a month. Small standing darshinis often **don't list**, or list at higher menu prices (Wikipedia notes delivery pushed some to raise prices). [S]
- **Competition in Jayanagar** [E + S]: legacy places (Vidyarthi Bhavan in Gandhi Bazaar/Basavanagudi nearby, Brahmin's Coffee Bar in Shankarapuram, Jayanagar's own MTR-era and SLV-type darshinis), new "corporate" darshinis (Rameshwaram Cafe, Bengaluru Cafe, Taaza Thindi in Jayanagar 4th T Block, South Kitchen) that use Toyota-style process control, Instagram reels and kiosks [S Marcellus]. Brand-new outlets compete on "ghee podi idli", "benne dose" and Instagram.

### 2.6 Cash flow and break-even (illustrative, [E])
- 500 sq ft, ~600–800 bills/day × ₹90 average ticket ≈ ₹55–70k/day ≈ **₹17–21 lakh/month** revenue at a good location.
- Food cost 35–40%, staff 20–25% (rising after the wage hike), rent 6–10%, LPG+power+water 6–8%, misc/maintenance 3–5%, GST 5% passed on. Net margin 8–15% when it works.
- Break-even typically takes **12–24 months** to recover capital. Most failures happen in the first year.
- **Why darshinis fail** [E + S]: rent too high or landlord hikes it, wrong side of the road or no parking, staff leaving (especially a good cook), inconsistent taste, partners fighting, input cost shocks (LPG 2026, milk, wages), undercapitalisation, a chain opening nearby, licence trouble (sealing for the nameboard or no trade licence), delivery-app losses.

### 2.7 Hygiene and inspections [E + S]
- FSSAI Schedule 4: clean water, pest control, hairnets/caps, gloves, separate veg washing, covered dustbins, staff medical certificates, FoSTaC-trained supervisor.
- Typical inspection hits: open drains or no grease trap, flies, staff without caps, old oil reused (TPC), batter stored in open, a kitchen floor that isn't washable, no FSSAI number displayed, a non-Kannada board.

## 4. How people really talk: sample exchanges (Bengaluru spoken)

Register notes: spoken Bengaluru Kannada drops vowels and endings (ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ → **ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ**, ಹೋಗಬೇಕು → **ಹೋಗ್ಬೇಕು**, ಇದ್ದೀರಾ → **ಇದೀರಾ**), and mixes English nouns freely (license, GST, stock, rent, advance, bill, delivery, quality, staff, salary). Kannada verbs plus English nouns is the norm: "ಅಪ್ಲೈ ಮಾಡಿ", "ರಿನೀವ್ ಮಾಡ್ಸಿ", "ಅಡ್ಜಸ್ಟ್ ಮಾಡ್ಕೊಳ್ಳಿ".

**Landlord bargaining (Jayanagar, an older homeowner leasing the ground floor):**
- ಓನರ್: ರೆಂಟ್ ಒಂದು ಲಕ್ಷ, ಹತ್ತು ತಿಂಗಳ ಅಡ್ವಾನ್ಸ್. ಹೋಟೆಲ್ ಅಂದ್ರೆ ನಮಗೆ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಭಯ, ಹೊಗೆ, ಜಿರಳೆ...
  *Rent is one lakh, ten months' advance. A hotel worries us a bit: smoke, cockroaches…*
- ನಾಯಕ: ಸಾರ್, ತೊಂಬತ್ತು ಮಾಡ್ಕೊಳ್ಳಿ, ಅಡ್ವಾನ್ಸ್ ಆರು ತಿಂಗಳು. ಚಿಮಣಿ ಮೇಲೆ ತನಕ ಹಾಕ್ತೀವಿ, ಪೆಸ್ಟ್ ಕಂಟ್ರೋಲ್ ತಿಂಗಳಿಗೊಂದ್ಸಲ.
  *Sir, make it ninety and six months' advance. We'll run the chimney up to the roof and do pest control once a month.*
- ಓನರ್: ಆಗಲ್ಲ ಅಪ್ಪ. ಎಂಟು ತಿಂಗಳು ಕೊಡಿ, ವರ್ಷಕ್ಕೆ ಐದು ಪರ್ಸೆಂಟ್ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ.
  *Can't do that, son. Give eight months, and five percent more each year.*

**Supplier (KR Market, 4:30 a.m.):**
- ನಾಯಕ: ಈರುಳ್ಳಿ ಕೆಜಿಗೆ ಎಷ್ಟು ಅಣ್ಣ?
  *How much is onion per kg, anna?*
- ವ್ಯಾಪಾರಿ: ನಲವತ್ತು. ಮೂಟೆ ತಗೊಂಡ್ರೆ ಮೂವತ್ತಾರು.
  *Forty. Thirty-six if you take a sack.*
- ನಾಯಕ: ಮೂವತ್ತೆರಡು ಹಾಕಿ, ದಿನಾ ತಗೊಳ್ತೀನಿ.
  *Make it thirty-two, I'll buy every day.*
- ವ್ಯಾಪಾರಿ: ಆಗಲ್ಲ ಸಾರ್, ಮಳೆ, ಮಾಲು ಬರ್ತಿಲ್ಲ. ಮೂವತ್ನಾಲ್ಕು ಫೈನಲ್.
  *Can't, sir. It's the rain, stock isn't coming in. Thirty-four, final.*

**Customer complaint:**
- ಗಿರಾಕಿ: ಏನ್ರೀ ಇದು, ಮಸಾಲೆ ದೋಸೆ ನೂರು ರೂಪಾಯಿ, ಸಾಂಬಾರ್ ತಣ್ಣಗಿದೆ!
  *What's this? A hundred rupees for masala dose, and the sambar is cold!*
- ಕೌಂಟರ್: ಸಾರಿ ಸಾರ್, ಬಿಸಿ ಸಾಂಬಾರ್ ಕೊಡ್ತೀನಿ. ಗ್ಯಾಸ್, ಹಾಲು ಎಲ್ಲಾ ರೇಟ್ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ ಆಗಿದೆ ಸಾರ್, ಏನ್ ಮಾಡೋದು.
  *Sorry sir, I'll give you hot sambar. Gas, milk, every price has gone up, sir, what can we do.*

**Motivating staff after a cook threatens to leave for a chain:**
- ಕುಕ್: ಅಲ್ಲಿ ಮೂವತ್ತೈದು ಕೊಡ್ತಾರಂತೆ ಸಾರ್.
  *They say they're paying thirty-five there, sir.*
- ನಾಯಕ: ಭಟ್ರೇ, ನಿಮ್ಮ ಕೈ ರುಚಿಯಿಂದಾನೇ ಜನ ಬರ್ತಾರೆ. ಮೂವತ್ತೆರಡು ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ, ದೀಪಾವಳಿಗೆ ಬೋನಸ್. ಆರು ತಿಂಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ಪಾಲು ಕೊಡೋದನ್ನೂ ಯೋಚನೆ ಮಾಡೋಣ.
  *Bhattre, people come because of the taste of your cooking. I'll make it thirty-two, with a Deepavali bonus. In six months let's also think about giving you a share.*

**Inspector visit (indirect hints only; story shows the owner choosing the legal route):**
- ಇನ್ಸ್‌ಪೆಕ್ಟರ್: ಟ್ರೇಡ್ ಲೈಸೆನ್ಸ್, FSSAI ತೋರಿಸಿ… ಗ್ರೀಸ್ ಟ್ರ್ಯಾಪ್ ಇಲ್ಲ, ಬೋರ್ಡ್‌ನಲ್ಲಿ ಕನ್ನಡ ಚಿಕ್ಕದಾಗಿದೆ. ನೋಟಿಸ್ ಕೊಡ್ಬೇಕಾಗುತ್ತೆ.
  *Show me the trade licence and FSSAI… There's no grease trap, and the Kannada on the board is too small. I'll have to issue a notice.*
- ಇನ್ಸ್‌ಪೆಕ್ಟರ್: ಹೊಸಬರು ಅನ್ಸುತ್ತೆ… ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಹೇಗೆ ನಡೆಯುತ್ತೆ ಗೊತ್ತಲ್ವಾ? ನಮ್ಮ ಮೇಲಿನವರಿಗೂ ಉತ್ತರ ಕೊಡ್ಬೇಕು. ಏನಾದ್ರೂ ಅಡ್ಜಸ್ಟ್ ಮಾಡ್ಕೊಳ್ಳೋಣ.
  *Seems you're new… You know how things work here, right? I have to answer to my superiors too. Let's adjust something.*
- ನಾಯಕ: ಸಾರ್, ಏನು ಸರಿ ಮಾಡ್ಬೇಕೋ ಬರೆದು ಕೊಡಿ, ಒಂದು ವಾರದಲ್ಲಿ ರೂಲ್ಸ್ ಪ್ರಕಾರ ಮಾಡ್ತೀವಿ. ದಂಡ ಇದ್ರೆ ರಶೀದಿ ಕೊಡಿ, ಕಟ್ತೀನಿ.
  *Sir, write down whatever needs fixing and we'll do it by the rules within a week. If there's a fine, give me a receipt and I'll pay.*
- ಇನ್ಸ್‌ಪೆಕ್ಟರ್: (pause) …ಸರಿ, ಒಂದು ವಾರ. ಮತ್ತೆ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ.
  *…Fine, one week. I'll come back.*

  Cultural note: offering coffee ("ಕಾಫಿ ತಗೊಳ್ಳಿ ಸಾರ್") is normal hospitality. Hints come as "ಅಡ್ಜಸ್ಟ್", "ನೋಡ್ಕೊಳ್ಳಿ", "ಮಾಮೂಲಿ", "ಖರ್ಚು-ವೆಚ್ಚ". Asking for a written notice or receipt (ರಶೀದಿ) is the polite way to decline.

**Family dinner:**
- ಅಮ್ಮ: ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಲ್ಲಿ ಎಷ್ಟು ಹೋಟೆಲ್ ಮುಚ್ಚಿಹೋದ್ವು ನೋಡಿದ್ಯಾ? ಗ್ಯಾಸ್ ಇಲ್ದೆ ಸೌದೆ ಉರಿಸ್ತಿದ್ರು!
  *Did you see how many hotels in Bengaluru shut down? They were burning firewood for lack of gas!*
- ಅಪ್ಪ: ಮನೆ ಅಡವಿಡೋದು ಬೇಡ. ಬೇಕಾದ್ರೆ ನನ್ನ FD ತಗೋ, ಮುದ್ರಾ ಲೋನ್ ನೋಡು.
  *No mortgaging the house. Take my FD if you need it, and look into a MUDRA loan.*

## 5. Lesson-design notes
- **Arc**: idea → family resistance → shop hunt (Jayanagar 4th Block vs side street) → landlord negotiation → licences (trade licence, FSSAI, GST, Kannada board) → equipment/LPG → hiring (Udupi bhattru + North Karnataka helper + Assamese dishwasher) → opening day 5 a.m. → first complaint → inspector → staff poaching → cash crunch (milk +₹8, wage order) → stabilises, small profit.
- Good **culture points**: tokens and standing; by-two coffee; Udupi heritage; 60% Kannada board (a language-identity issue); "BBMP" vs "GBA" naming confusion; LPG/firewood 2026; the darshini vs Instagram-cafe divide.
- Have learners notice which words are **English in real speech** (rent, advance, license, stock, bill, parcel, token, GST, quality, delivery, salary, staff, interior, agreement) vs which stay **Kannada** (ಬಾಡಿಗೆ is still common; ಸಾಲ, ಲಾಭ, ನಷ್ಟ, ಖರ್ಚು, ಬಾಕಿ, ಲೆಕ್ಕ, ಗಿರಾಕಿ, ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ, ಹಿಟ್ಟು, ರುಬ್ಬು, ಒಗ್ಗರಣೆ are everyday Kannada).
- Formal words (ಪರವಾನಗಿ, ನೋಂದಣಿ, ತಪಾಸಣೆ, ನಾಮಫಲಕ, ಉದ್ದಿಮೆ ಪರವಾನಗಿ) appear on **forms, notices and the news**, so learners need them for reading, but people say the English.
